When a refinery or chemical plant hands you a certificate requirement spec, the liability limits can be jarring if you've only worked general commercial construction. $5M. $10M. $25M in combined liability. That's not a GL policy — that's a GL policy plus an umbrella stack.

Commercial umbrella insurance is the mechanism industrial contractors use to reach the liability limits industrial facilities require, without buying primary GL at those limits (which would be prohibitively expensive). Here's how it works, what it costs, and how to structure it correctly.

## What a Commercial Umbrella Does

A commercial umbrella policy sits above your existing underlying liability policies — your GL, commercial auto, and employers' liability (Part Two of workers' comp). It provides excess coverage when an underlying policy's per-occurrence or aggregate limit is exhausted.

If your GL has a $1M per occurrence limit and a $2M aggregate, and a $3M claim arises, your GL pays $1M and your $2M umbrella pays the next $2M — up to the umbrella's per-occurrence limit. Without the umbrella, you're responsible for the $2M above your GL limit.

The umbrella extends over all of your underlying policies simultaneously — one umbrella policy replaces three separate excess policies (excess GL, excess auto, excess employers' liability).

## What Industrial Facility Owners Typically Require

Requirements vary significantly by facility type, project size, and the plant owner's risk management policy. Common ranges:

**Smaller industrial maintenance projects** — $2M–$5M in combined liability. May be met with primary GL alone or a small umbrella.

**Standard refinery and chemical plant projects** — $5M–$10M combined. Typically requires a primary GL at $1M–$2M plus an umbrella to the required limit.

**Major turnarounds and capital projects at large facilities** — $10M–$25M combined. Requires a coordinated primary + umbrella structure, sometimes with an excess umbrella above the first umbrella layer.

**Government and critical infrastructure projects** — Can exceed $25M. May require multiple umbrella layers.

The certificate requirement spec will tell you exactly what combined per-occurrence and aggregate limits you need across all underlying policies. We review the spec and structure the program to meet it.

## Primary GL First, Then Umbrella

The most cost-effective structure is a $1M or $2M primary GL, with umbrella making up the difference to the required combined limit.

**Example:** A plant owner requires $10M combined liability.
- Primary GL: $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate — insures the first $1M per occurrence
- Commercial umbrella: $9M — brings per-occurrence coverage to $10M

Buying a $10M primary GL policy directly would cost far more than a $1M primary plus a $9M umbrella. Umbrella premiums are structured as excess layers and are priced accordingly — the cost per million of umbrella coverage is much lower than primary GL.

## Umbrella vs. Excess CPL

Here's a critical point that industrial contractors often miss: standard commercial umbrellas do NOT extend over contractors pollution liability (CPL).

A commercial umbrella typically sits over GL, commercial auto, and employers' liability. CPL is a specialty policy, not a standard underlying policy, and most umbrella policies exclude coverage for pollution claims even if CPL is listed in the underlying schedule.

If your plant owner requires high-limit CPL in addition to high-limit GL, the solution is:
1. Primary CPL at the per-occurrence limit you want
2. A separate excess CPL (or excess environmental) policy above it

We structure both when facility requirements call for high-limit pollution coverage.

## Drop-Down Coverage

When a primary GL aggregate is exhausted during the policy year — typically by multiple smaller claims — the umbrella "drops down" to maintain primary-equivalent coverage for the remainder of the year.

For industrial contractors running multiple simultaneous projects, aggregate erosion is a real risk. A turnaround project, a pipeline project, and two ongoing maintenance projects can generate enough claim activity to erode a $2M primary aggregate. Drop-down protection ensures you're not left with zero first-dollar coverage for the rest of the year.

## What Umbrella Underwriters Look at for Industrial Contractors

Industrial umbrella underwriting considers the hazard profile of your work more closely than standard commercial umbrella.

**Underlying policy structure** — Umbrella underwriters review your GL, auto, and workers' comp to confirm they're written on appropriate forms with appropriate limits. An umbrella won't sit over inadequate underlying policies.

**Work type and facility exposure** — Refinery and chemical plant work, pipeline projects, and high-hazard industrial maintenance require specialty umbrella markets. Standard commercial umbrella underwriters may decline or restrict industrial facility coverage.

**Loss history** — Large prior claims, especially those that approached policy limits, create umbrella underwriting scrutiny. Industrial accident history is reviewed carefully.

**Operations description** — What you do, where you do it, and what kinds of incidents are realistically possible drives the umbrella underwriter's pricing and terms.
